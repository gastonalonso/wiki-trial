**Ha Long Bay** is an inlet of the Gulf of Tonkin in northeastern Vietnam, famous for roughly 1,600 limestone islands and islets. It is a World Heritage Site and one of the most visited places in the country.

## Karst landscape

The islands are the remains of a limestone plateau that has been shaped by rain and groundwater over millions of years. Limestone dissolves slowly in rainwater, so surfaces develop towers, cliffs and hollows, a landscape called karst. When the sea level rose after the last ice age, the water flooded the lowland between the towers and left the peaks standing as islands. Many islands contain [caves](../../physical/landforms/caves/), and some have hidden lagoons.

## Climate

The bay has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and wet because of the [monsoon](../../physical/climate/monsoons/), and winters are cooler and drier. Typhoons, a regional name for [tropical cyclones](../../physical/climate/tropical-cyclones/), occasionally cross the area in the warmer months.

## Life and people

The waters shelter corals, fish and mangroves, and some islands have forest with rare species. Communities have long lived on floating villages in the bay, fishing and farming, although many have moved ashore in recent years. Tourism boats, pollution and coastal development are the main pressures.

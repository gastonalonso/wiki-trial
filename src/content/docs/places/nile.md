The **Nile** flows roughly 6,650 kilometres from East Africa to the Mediterranean Sea and is often described as the longest river in the world, although the [Amazon](../amazon-rainforest/) is sometimes given a longer length depending on how the source is measured.[^1] It flows north, which is unusual for a major river.

## Sources and course

The Nile has two main branches. The White Nile rises in the lake region of central Africa and flows out of Lake Victoria, a lake shared by Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania. The Blue Nile starts in the highlands of [Ethiopia](../../world/africa/ethiopia/) and supplies most of the water and silt that reach Egypt during the flood season. The two meet at Khartoum in Sudan. From there the river crosses the Sahara with almost no tributaries.

## Delta and floodplain

Near the sea the river splits into branches and forms a broad [delta](../../physical/water/deltas/). The fertile strip along the river and in the delta supports most of [Egypt](../../world/africa/egypt/)'s population. Before the Aswan High Dam was completed in 1970, annual floods deposited fresh silt on the fields; the dam now controls the flow and stores water in Lake Nasser.

## Human importance

Ancient Egyptian civilisation grew along the Nile, using its floods for farming and its channel for transport. Today several countries share the water, and reservoir projects upstream are a source of disagreement. For more on how rivers work, see [rivers](../../physical/water/rivers/).

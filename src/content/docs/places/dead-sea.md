The **Dead Sea** is a landlocked salt lake in the Jordan Rift Valley, bordered by Jordan to the east and by Israel and the West Bank to the west. Its shore lies more than 430 metres below sea level, the lowest point on land on Earth.[^1]

## Salt and water

The river Jordan flows into the lake, but no river flows out. Water leaves only by evaporation in the hot, dry climate, which leaves the salts behind. As a result the water is roughly nine or ten times as salty as the ocean, so that swimmers float easily and few animals can live in it. It is a good example of an endorheic, or closed, lake; see [lakes](../../physical/water/lakes/).

## Shrinking shoreline

The lake has been shrinking for several decades. Water from the Jordan and its tributaries is used for farming and drinking, and mineral companies draw water from the lake for evaporation ponds. The water level has fallen by more than a metre a year in recent decades, and sinkholes have opened along the shore.

| Feature | Approximate figure |
| --- | --- |
| Shore elevation | About 430 m below sea level |
| Salinity | About 30 to 35 percent |
| Maximum depth | Roughly 300 m |

## Climate and use

The area is a hot [desert](../../physical/landforms/deserts/), with very little rain. Minerals, mud and the spa tourism that grows around them are economically important, and the dry air and thick atmosphere at this depth attract visitors.

:::caution
Getting the salty water in the eyes or on cuts is painful, and swallowing it can be dangerous.
:::

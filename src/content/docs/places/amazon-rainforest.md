The **Amazon rainforest** fills most of the Amazon River basin in northern South America. Around 60 percent of it lies in [Brazil](../../world/south-america/brazil/), and large parts are in [Peru](../../world/south-america/peru/), with smaller shares in several other countries. It is the largest area of tropical rainforest in the world.

## Climate and structure

The forest lies close to the equator, so temperatures stay warm all year and rain falls in most months. Total annual rainfall is commonly 2,000 mm or more. Tall trees form a closed canopy, below which sit an understorey and a dim forest floor. The general features of this biome are described under [tropical rainforest](../../physical/biomes/tropical-rainforest/).

## Biodiversity

The Amazon holds a large share of the planet's known plant and animal species, including many that live nowhere else. Insects, birds, fish and mammals such as jaguars and river dolphins all depend on the forest and its waterways. Seasonal flooding turns large areas into flooded forest, which fish use for feeding and breeding.

## The river

The Amazon is among the largest rivers in the world by volume of water, discharging more than any other river into the ocean. Its [tributaries](../../physical/water/rivers/) include many that are themselves among the world's longest rivers. The seasonal rise and fall of the water can be many metres.

## People and pressures

Indigenous peoples have lived in the region for thousands of years, and many communities still depend on the forest. Clearing for cattle pasture, farming, roads and mining has removed a notable portion of the forest. Because the trees store carbon and release water vapour, the loss is linked to wider concerns discussed under [climate change](../../physical/climate/climate-change/).

The **Great Barrier Reef** stretches for more than 2,000 kilometres along the northeastern coast of [Australia](../../world/oceania/australia/) in the Coral Sea. It is made up of thousands of individual reefs and hundreds of islands, and it is large enough to be seen from space.

## Structure

The reef is built by tiny animals called coral polyps, which secrete limestone skeletons. Over thousands of years these skeletons accumulate into reef structures. The Great Barrier Reef includes fringing reefs close to the shore, patch reefs and outer barrier reefs, separated from the mainland by a shallow lagoon. The general processes are described under [coral reefs](../../physical/biomes/coral-reefs/).

## Life on the reef

Corals live in partnership with algae that provide them with food through photosynthesis, which is why reefs grow in clear, warm, sunlit water. The reef supports many hundreds of coral species, plus fish, turtles, sharks, rays and seabirds. It is also visited by humpback whales.

## Threats

When seawater becomes too warm, corals expel their algae and turn white in an event called **bleaching**. Several major bleaching events have occurred in recent decades. Other pressures include run-off from farmland, outbreaks of crown-of-thorns starfish and damage from [tropical cyclones](../../physical/climate/tropical-cyclones/). Rising ocean temperatures link these problems to [climate change](../../physical/climate/climate-change/).

:::caution
Bleached coral is not necessarily dead, but it can starve if warm conditions last. Reefs can recover only if the water cools in time.
:::

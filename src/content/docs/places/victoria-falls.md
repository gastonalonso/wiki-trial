**Victoria Falls** is a waterfall on the Zambezi River, where the river forms the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe. It is about 1,700 metres wide and drops roughly 100 metres into a narrow gorge. It is not the tallest or the widest waterfall in the world, but it is often described as the largest sheet of falling water.

## The falls and the gorge

The Zambezi flows across a flat basalt plateau and plunges into a long crack, a fault line in the rock. Spray rises high above the gorge and can be seen from far away. Local people call the falls Mosi-oa-Tunya, which means "the smoke that thunders". Over thousands of years the falls have worked their way upstream, leaving a zigzag chain of gorges below. See [waterfalls](../../physical/water/waterfalls/) for how falls form.

## Seasons

The flow depends on the rainy season in the upper basin. The river is at its highest between about February and May, when so much spray is produced that the view can be hidden, and at its lowest in the dry season, when parts of the cliff are exposed. The surrounding region has a [savanna](../../physical/biomes/savanna/) climate with a wet and dry season.

## Wildlife and tourism

A small rainforest, kept green by the spray, grows beside the falls. Elephants, hippos and crocodiles live along the river upstream. Tourism, including viewing, bungee jumping and river rafting, is a major source of income for both countries.

:::tip[Best time to visit]
For the most water, visit in the months after the rains. For clear views of the cliff and gorge, the dry season is better.
:::

The **Sahara** covers roughly 9 million square kilometres, an area comparable to that of the United States. It extends across North Africa from the Atlantic coast to the Red Sea and passes through about eleven countries, including [Egypt](../../world/africa/egypt/) and [Morocco](../../world/africa/morocco/).

## Landscape

Contrary to common images, only a minority of the Sahara is covered by sand dunes. Large sand seas called ergs exist, but much of the surface is bare rock plateau, gravel plain and dry riverbed. Mountains such as the Tibesti and Ahaggar rise well above the plains. See [deserts](../../physical/landforms/deserts/) and [plateaus](../../physical/landforms/plateaus/) for more on these landforms.

## Climate

The Sahara lies under a belt of descending dry air near the tropics, so rainfall is very low and falls irregularly. Daytime temperatures in summer often exceed 40 °C, while nights, especially in winter, can be cold. The desert is described under [climate zones](../../physical/climate/climate-zones/) as a hot arid climate.

| Feature | Typical condition |
| --- | --- |
| Annual rainfall | Under 100 mm in most areas |
| Summer daytime temperature | 35 to 45 °C |
| Winter night temperature | Can approach freezing |

## Water and life

Plants and animals survive through adaptations such as deep roots, nocturnal activity and the ability to go long periods without water. Oases occur where [groundwater](../../physical/water/groundwater/) reaches the surface, and they have supported settlements and caravan routes for thousands of years. The Nile is the one major river to cross the desert, in the east.

:::note
The Sahara was much wetter about 6,000 to 10,000 years ago, with lakes and grassland. Rock art in the central mountains shows animals that no longer live there.
:::

**Uluru**, also known as Ayers Rock, is a large rock formation in the Northern Territory in the middle of [Australia](../../world/oceania/australia/). It stands about 340 metres above the surrounding plain and measures roughly 9 kilometres around its base.

## Geology

Uluru is made of arkose, a coarse sandstone, laid down in an ancient basin hundreds of millions of years ago. The layers were later tilted until they stand almost vertical. Because the surrounding rock wore away faster, the hard mass was left standing as an isolated hill, a type sometimes called an inselberg. Much of the rock is hidden underground, and the visible part is only a portion of the whole.

## Colour and climate

The rock appears to change colour from orange to deep red during the day, especially at sunrise and sunset, because iron minerals on the surface have rusted. The area is a hot [desert](../../physical/landforms/deserts/) with low and unreliable rainfall, though heavy storms can create waterfalls that run down the rock. The land around is a flat [plain](../../physical/landforms/plains/).

## Cultural meaning

Uluru is sacred to the Anangu, the traditional owners, and is part of Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park. Ownership was returned to the Anangu in 1985 and the park is jointly managed. Climbing the rock was permanently closed in 2019.

:::note
Uluru is a World Heritage Site for both its natural and its cultural values.
:::

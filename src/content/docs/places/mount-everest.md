**Mount Everest** rises about 8,849 metres above sea level on the border between Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region of China. It is the highest point on Earth measured from sea level and part of the Himalayan range.

## Formation

The Himalayas began to rise roughly 50 million years ago, when the Indian plate collided with the Eurasian plate. Because neither plate sinks easily, the crust was pushed upward and folded, and the range is still rising by a few millimetres a year. Much of the summit rock is marine limestone, which formed on an ancient sea floor.[^1]

## Climate and glaciers

At the summit the air pressure is about a third of that at sea level, and winter temperatures fall far below -30 °C. Winds from the west can be extremely strong in winter, while the summer monsoon brings heavy snow. The mountain feeds large [glaciers](../../physical/landforms/glaciers/), such as the Khumbu Glacier, which is a source of meltwater for rivers in the region. See [monsoons](../../physical/climate/monsoons/) for the seasonal winds involved.

## Climbing and people

The first confirmed ascent was made in 1953 by Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay. Most climbers now start from the Nepalese side, supported by Sherpa guides whose communities live in the valleys below. Crowding, waste and the risk of avalanches are lasting concerns.

:::tip[Two names, two heights]
Everest is called Sagarmatha in Nepali and Chomolungma in Tibetan. Nepal and China agreed on a joint height figure in 2020.
:::

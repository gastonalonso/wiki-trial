**Lake Baikal** lies in southern Siberia in Russia, north of the border with [Mongolia](../../world/asia/mongolia/). It is the deepest lake in the world, with a maximum depth of about 1,640 metres, and it holds roughly a fifth of the world's unfrozen fresh surface water.

## Formation

Baikal fills a crescent-shaped rift valley, where the continental crust is slowly pulling apart. This process has gone on for many millions of years, which makes it likely the oldest lake on Earth. The lake is about 600 kilometres long but only about 80 kilometres across at its widest point. More than 300 rivers flow into it, the largest being the Selenga, which comes from Mongolia. Only one river, the Angara, flows out.

## Water and ice

Baikal's water is cold and very clear. In winter the surface freezes to a thickness of about a metre, and the ice is strong enough to carry vehicles. See [lakes](../../physical/water/lakes/) for the general features of lakes.

## Wildlife

The lake is home to a large number of species found nowhere else, including the Baikal seal, one of the few freshwater seals in the world, and many small crustaceans. The surrounding hills are covered by [taiga](../../physical/biomes/taiga/), the northern coniferous forest.

:::note
Baikal is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its deep, oxygen-rich water is unusual for such a large lake.
:::

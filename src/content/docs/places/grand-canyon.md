The **Grand Canyon** in northern Arizona, in the [United States](../../world/north-america/united-states/), is about 450 kilometres long and up to roughly 1.8 kilometres deep. It is among the best-known examples of a [canyon](../../physical/landforms/canyons/) anywhere in the world.

## How it formed

The Colorado River began cutting into the Colorado [Plateau](../../physical/landforms/plateaus/) several million years ago, as the plateau was slowly uplifted. The river carries sand and gravel that wear down the bed, while rain, frost and landslides widen the walls. The result is a deep gorge with stepped cliffs.

## Layers of rock

The canyon walls expose layers of sedimentary rock laid down over hundreds of millions of years, with much older rock at the bottom. Geologists read these layers to learn about ancient seas, deserts and rivers.

| Level | Rock character |
| --- | --- |
| Rim | Pale limestone |
| Middle walls | Red sandstone and shale |
| Inner gorge | Dark, very old crystalline rock |

## Climate and life

The rim is high and cool, with pine forest and winter snow, while the canyon floor is hot desert. These differences in height create several [climate zones](../../physical/climate/climate-zones/) within a short distance. The canyon is part of a national park and a World Heritage Site.

:::tip[Visiting]
The South Rim is open all year and is the most visited part. Summer heat at the bottom of the canyon can be dangerous, so hikers should carry plenty of water.
:::

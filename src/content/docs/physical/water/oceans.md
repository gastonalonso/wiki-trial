The **oceans** are the vast, connected bodies of salt water that cover about 71 percent of the Earth. Although geographers recognise a single **world ocean**, it is divided into named basins by the continents. Together they hold roughly 97 percent of all water on the planet and regulate its climate by absorbing and moving heat.

## The five oceans

| Ocean | Approximate area (million km²) | Greatest depth (m) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pacific | 165 | about 10,900 (Challenger Deep) |
| Atlantic | 106 | about 8,400 (Puerto Rico Trench) |
| Indian | 70 | about 7,200 (Java Trench) |
| Southern | 20 | about 7,200 (South Sandwich Trench) |
| Arctic | 15 | about 5,500 |

The Pacific is larger than all the land on Earth combined. The Southern Ocean, which surrounds [Antarctica](../../../world/antarctica/), was officially recognised by some bodies only in recent decades, and its northern limit is defined by a current rather than a coast.[^1]

## Ocean structure

Near the surface, sunlight warms the water and supports most marine life. Below a few hundred metres it grows dark and cold. The sea floor is not flat: it has mid-ocean ridges, vast plains and deep **trenches**. The deepest point, in the [Mariana Trench](../../../places/mariana-trench/), lies nearly 11 kilometres down. Ocean water is salty, with an average salinity of about 3.5 percent, because rivers have carried dissolved minerals to the sea for millions of years.

## Movement

Wind drives surface currents, while differences in temperature and salinity drive slow, deep circulation. These systems carry warm water toward the poles and cold water toward the equator, which strongly affects [climate](../../climate/weather-and-climate/). See [ocean currents](../../climate/ocean-currents/). Tides, caused mainly by the Moon's gravity, raise and lower the sea level twice a day in most places.

:::tip[Did you know?]
Humans have explored only a small fraction of the sea floor in detail; maps of the surface of Mars and the Moon are often more complete.
:::

A **lake** is a body of water, usually fresh, that fills a hollow in the land and is surrounded by shore. Lakes are found on every continent and range from small ponds to inland seas. Together they hold a large share of the planet's accessible surface fresh water, and they are important for drinking water, fishing, transport and recreation.

## How lakes form

Lakes form in many ways. [Glaciers](../../landforms/glaciers/) scoop out basins and leave dams of debris, which created the Great Lakes of North America and thousands of lakes across [Canada](../../../world/north-america/canada/). Movement of the crust forms **rift lakes**, such as [Lake Baikal](../../../places/lake-baikal/) and the lakes of East Africa; Baikal is the deepest lake and holds about a fifth of the world's unfrozen surface fresh water. Volcanoes leave crater lakes, and rivers leave **oxbow lakes** when a meander is cut off.

## Largest lakes

| --- | --- | --- |
| Caspian Sea | 371,000 | Russia, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, Iran, Azerbaijan |
| Superior | 82,000 | Canada, United States |

| Huron | 59,600 | Canada, United States |
| Michigan | 58,000 | United States |

The Caspian Sea is salty and is classed as a lake because it is landlocked.

## Salt lakes and drying lakes

Lakes with no outlet lose water only by evaporation and become salty, like the [Dead Sea](../../../places/dead-sea/). Such lakes are very sensitive to change. The shrinking of the Aral Sea shows what happens when the rivers that feed a lake are diverted.

:::note
Lake Victoria, the largest lake in Africa, is a major source of the White Nile. See [rivers](../rivers/).
:::

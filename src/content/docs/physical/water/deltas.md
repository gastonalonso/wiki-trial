A **delta** is a landform created where a river slows as it enters a larger body of water, such as a sea or lake, and drops the sediment it carries. The deposit often takes a triangular shape, which gave the feature its name from the Greek letter delta. Deltas are flat, fertile and rich in wildlife, and several of the world's most crowded regions are built on them.

## How deltas form

As a [river](../rivers/) reaches the sea, its current slows and it loses the energy needed to carry sand and silt. The load settles, blocks the channel and forces the river to split into smaller branches called **distributaries**. Over time, the delta grows seaward. Its shape depends on the balance between river, waves and tides:

- **River-dominated** deltas, like the Mississippi, extend in long fingers.
- **Wave-dominated** deltas, like the Nile, are smoothed into a curved front.
- **Tide-dominated** deltas, like the Ganges-Brahmaputra, are cut by wide tidal channels.

## Major deltas

The Nile Delta in [Egypt](../../../world/africa/egypt/) holds most of the country's farmland and a very large share of its people. The Ganges-Brahmaputra delta, shared by India and Bangladesh, is the largest in the world and contains the Sundarbans, a vast [mangrove](../../biomes/mangroves/) forest. Other major deltas include the Mekong, the Mississippi, the Yangtze and the Lena in Siberia, which is the largest Arctic delta.

## Threats

Deltas depend on a steady supply of sediment. Dams upstream trap silt, while pumping groundwater and drilling for oil make the land sink, and rising seas push salt water inland. Many deltas are therefore shrinking or at risk of flooding. See [climate change](../../climate/climate-change/) and [groundwater](../groundwater/).

:::caution
Because deltas are low and flat, even a small rise in sea level can flood large areas and make soils too salty for crops.
:::

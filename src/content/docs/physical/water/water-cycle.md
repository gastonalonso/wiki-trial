The **water cycle**, or hydrological cycle, is the continuous circulation of water between the oceans, the atmosphere, the land and living things. The total amount of water on Earth changes very little, but it constantly moves between forms: liquid, ice and vapour. The Sun supplies the energy and gravity pulls the water back down.

## Stages of the cycle

1. **Evaporation**: heat from the Sun turns liquid water in [oceans](../oceans/), [lakes](../lakes/) and soil into vapour. Most evaporation comes from the oceans.
2. **Transpiration**: plants release water vapour through their leaves. Together with evaporation this is called evapotranspiration.
3. **Condensation**: as moist air rises and cools, vapour condenses into droplets and forms clouds.
4. **Precipitation**: water falls as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
5. **Runoff and infiltration**: some water flows over land into [rivers](../rivers/), and some soaks into the ground and becomes [groundwater](../groundwater/).
6. **Storage**: water is held for long periods in ice, lakes, aquifers and the sea.

## Stores and timescales

Water stays in different places for very different lengths of time. A water molecule may spend only days in the atmosphere, weeks to months in a river, years in a lake and thousands of years in a deep aquifer, an ocean basin or a large ice sheet. Ice in [glaciers](../../landforms/glaciers/) and polar ice sheets holds about two thirds of the fresh water, so changes in ice can shift the cycle on a large scale.

## The cycle and climate

The water cycle moves heat as well as water. Evaporation absorbs energy from the surface, and condensation releases it into the air, which fuels storms and [tropical cyclones](../../climate/tropical-cyclones/). Patterns of rainfall help define [climate zones](../../climate/climate-zones/) and seasonal systems such as [monsoons](../../climate/monsoons/). As the planet warms, more water evaporates, which tends to intensify heavy rain in some regions and drought in others. See [climate change](../../climate/climate-change/).

:::tip[Did you know?]
The water you drink today has been recycled for billions of years; no new water is made by the cycle, it simply moves.
:::

A **river** is a natural channel of flowing fresh water, normally draining toward an ocean, sea or lake. Rivers collect water from rain, snowmelt and springs across an area called a **drainage basin** or watershed, and they have been the main routes for transport, farming and settlement through human history.

## Course of a river

The **source** is often a spring or a melting glacier in the highlands. In the upper course the river is steep and fast and cuts narrow [valleys](../../landforms/valleys/), sometimes forming [waterfalls](../waterfalls/). In the middle course it is wider and picks up tributaries. In the lower course it slows, swings in broad bends called **meanders** and deposits silt across its floodplain. At the **mouth**, it enters the sea through an [estuary](../estuaries/) or builds a [delta](../deltas/).

## Longest rivers

| River | Approximate length (km) | Flows into |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nile | 6,650 | Mediterranean Sea |
| Amazon | 6,400 | Atlantic Ocean |
| Yangtze | 6,300 | East China Sea |
| Mississippi-Missouri | 6,200 | Gulf of Mexico |
| Yenisei-Angara | 5,500 | Kara Sea |

Lengths depend on where the source and mouth are placed, so rankings differ, especially for the Nile and the Amazon.[^1] By volume of water, the Amazon is by far the largest river on Earth.

## Rivers and people

Early civilisations arose along the Nile in [Egypt](../../../world/africa/egypt/), the Yangtze and Yellow rivers in [China](../../../world/asia/china/) and the Indus and Ganges in [India](../../../world/asia/india/). Rivers supply drinking and irrigation water, hydropower and fish, but they also flood. See the pages on the [Nile](../../../places/nile/) and the [Amazon rainforest](../../../places/amazon-rainforest/).

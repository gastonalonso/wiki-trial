Water covers about 71 percent of the Earth's surface, and most of it is salt water in the oceans. The rest, fresh water, is held in ice, underground aquifers, lakes, rivers and the atmosphere. Water is never still on a planetary scale: it evaporates, falls as rain or snow, flows over and under the land and returns to the sea. This continuous circulation is the water cycle, and it links every feature described in this section.

Water shapes the land through erosion and deposition, supplies drinking water and irrigation, supports a huge share of the planet's life and has guided the location of nearly every major settlement. The articles below describe the main kinds of water feature. For the shape of the land that holds them, see [landforms](../landforms/), and for the atmospheric side of water, see [climate](../climate/) and the wider [physical geography](../) overview.

## Articles

- [Oceans](oceans/): the five great ocean basins that hold most of Earth's water.
- [Seas](seas/): smaller regions of salt water partly enclosed by land.
- [Rivers](rivers/): channels that carry fresh water from highlands to the sea, with a table of the longest.
- [Lakes](lakes/): inland bodies of standing water, with a table of the largest.
- [Waterfalls](waterfalls/): places where a river drops over a steep step.
- [Wetlands](wetlands/): marshes, swamps and bogs where land is saturated.
- [Deltas](deltas/): fan-shaped deposits where rivers meet a slower body of water.
- [Estuaries](estuaries/): partly enclosed coastal waters where river water mixes with the sea.
- [Groundwater](groundwater/): water stored in soil and rock beneath the surface.
- [Water cycle](water-cycle/): the continuous movement of water between sea, air and land.

title: Groundwater
description: Groundwater is water stored in soil and rock beneath the surface, held in aquifers and tapped by wells and springs.

**Groundwater** is water found in the spaces between soil particles and in the cracks and pores of rock below the surface. It is the largest reservoir of accessible fresh water on land, far greater than all lakes and rivers combined, and it supplies drinking water to billions of people and a large part of the water used for irrigation.

## How groundwater is stored

Rain and snowmelt soak into the ground and move downward until they reach rock where every space is filled with water. The top of this saturated zone is the **water table**. A body of rock that holds and transmits useful amounts of water is an **aquifer**. Typical aquifers are sand, gravel, sandstone and fractured or dissolved limestone. Layers of clay or dense rock can confine an aquifer, and water in a confined aquifer may be under pressure and rise on its own when tapped, forming an artesian well.

## Moving and emerging

Groundwater moves slowly, often only centimetres or metres a day, though it can flow quickly through caves in limestone; see [caves](../../landforms/caves/). Where the water table meets the surface, it emerges as a spring or feeds [rivers](../rivers/), [lakes](../lakes/) and [wetlands](../wetlands/). Rivers in dry periods are often sustained by groundwater. In [deserts](../../landforms/deserts/), an **oasis** forms where groundwater reaches the surface.

## Use and overuse

When people pump water out faster than rain refills it, water tables fall. This can dry up wells, shrink rivers and cause the ground to sink. The great aquifers of the Ogallala in the United States and the Nubian Sandstone beneath the [Sahara](../../../places/sahara/) hold water that accumulated over thousands of years, so they refill very slowly.[^1] Pollution is also a risk because contaminated groundwater is hard to clean.

## Role in the water cycle

Groundwater is an important link in the [water cycle](../water-cycle/): it stores water for long periods before releasing it to the surface or the sea.

:::note
Some aquifers hold fossil water that is thousands of years old, so using it can be more like mining than harvesting.
:::

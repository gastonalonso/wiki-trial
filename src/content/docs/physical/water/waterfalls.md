A **waterfall** is a place where a river or stream flows over a vertical or near-vertical drop. Waterfalls range from small cascades a few metres high to enormous curtains of water that can be heard for kilometres. They are among the most visited natural sights in the world, and many are protected as national parks.

## How waterfalls form

Most waterfalls form where a river crosses from hard rock to softer rock. The soft layer erodes faster and leaves a step, and the water plunging over it digs a **plunge pool** at the base. Waterfalls also occur where rivers leave a [plateau](../../landforms/plateaus/) edge, where earth movements create a scarp across the channel, and where a tributary flows from a **hanging valley** left by a glacier into a deeper [valley](../../landforms/valleys/).

## Retreat

Over time, the force of the water undercuts the hard cap rock, which eventually collapses. The falls therefore move upstream, leaving a deep gorge behind them. Niagara Falls has retreated several kilometres since the end of the last ice age.

## Notable waterfalls

- **Angel Falls** in Venezuela is the tallest uninterrupted waterfall, at about 980 metres.
- **Iguazu Falls**, on the border of [Argentina](../../../world/south-america/argentina/) and [Brazil](../../../world/south-america/brazil/), is a wide system of hundreds of separate drops.
- [Victoria Falls](../../../places/victoria-falls/), on the Zambezi, is one of the largest sheets of falling water, about 1.7 kilometres wide.
- **Niagara Falls** lies between [Canada](../../../world/north-america/canada/) and the United States.

Tallest and widest are different records, so a waterfall can be famous for height, width or volume of flow.

:::tip
The best time to see most waterfalls is just after the rainy season, when river flow is at its highest.
:::

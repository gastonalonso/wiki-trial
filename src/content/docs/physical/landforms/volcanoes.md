A **volcano** is an opening in the Earth's crust through which molten rock, ash and gases escape onto the surface. The molten rock is called **magma** while underground and **lava** once erupted. Over time, repeated eruptions pile up material to build a cone or dome, though some volcanoes are simply fissures or craters in flat ground.

## Where volcanoes occur

Most volcanoes lie along the boundaries of tectonic plates. The best known zone is the **Ring of Fire** around the Pacific Ocean, which holds a large share of the world's active volcanoes, including many in [Japan](../../../world/asia/japan/), [Indonesia](../../../world/asia/indonesia/), Chile and the western United States. Volcanoes also form at divergent boundaries, such as the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, which rises above the sea in [Iceland](../../../world/europe/iceland/). A third group sits over **hot spots**, plumes of unusually hot rock beneath a plate; the Hawaiian Islands are the classic example.

## Types of volcano

- **Shield volcanoes** have broad, gentle slopes built from runny lava, as in Hawaii.
- **Stratovolcanoes** (composite cones) are steep and built from alternating lava and ash; Mount Fuji is one.
- **Cinder cones** are small, steep cones of loose fragments.
- **Calderas** are large basins left when a volcano collapses after a major eruption.

## Hazards and benefits

Eruptions can produce lava flows, ash clouds, fast-moving flows of hot gas and rock, and mudflows called lahars. Ash can disrupt air travel across whole regions. Yet volcanic soils are often very fertile, and volcanic heat supplies geothermal energy, which Iceland uses widely for heating and electricity.

:::caution
A volcano that has been quiet for centuries is not necessarily extinct. Scientists classify volcanoes as active, dormant or extinct, but the boundaries are uncertain.
:::

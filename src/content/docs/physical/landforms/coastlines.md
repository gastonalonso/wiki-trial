title: Coastlines
description: Coastlines are where land meets sea, shaped by waves, tides and currents into cliffs, beaches, bays and other features.

A **coastline** is the boundary between land and sea. It is one of the most dynamic landforms, constantly reshaped by waves, tides, currents and storms, and also one of the most heavily populated. A large share of the world's people live near a coast, and many of the largest [cities](../../../human/cities/) are ports.

## Erosional coasts

Where the land is high or rock is hard, waves attack the base of a cliff, undercutting it until it collapses. Over time this produces a sequence of features: headlands and bays, then caves, arches, stacks and stumps as the sea cuts through the rock. The retreat leaves a flat **wave-cut platform** at the foot of the cliff. Sea [caves](../caves/) are one result. Dramatic cliff coasts include parts of the Atlantic coast of [France](../../../world/europe/france/).

## Depositional coasts

Where there is a supply of sediment and gentle waves, material builds up instead. Beaches, spits, bars and dunes form, and rivers deposit sediment to make [deltas](../../water/deltas/) and mudflats. Sheltered shores may develop [wetlands](../../water/wetlands/), salt marshes and mangrove forests; see [mangroves](../../biomes/mangroves/).

## Drowned and glacial coasts

When sea level rises or land sinks, the sea floods river and glacial valleys. A flooded river valley becomes a **ria**, as in southwest England and northern [Spain](../../../world/europe/spain/), and a flooded glacial valley becomes a **fjord**. [Norway](../../../world/europe/norway/) has one of the longest and most indented coastlines anywhere.

## Measuring a coastline

The length of a coast depends on the scale of the map used to measure it. A smaller ruler follows more twists and gives a greater total, so figures for national coastlines differ between sources.[^1] Canada is usually cited as having the longest coastline of any country, followed by Norway and Indonesia.

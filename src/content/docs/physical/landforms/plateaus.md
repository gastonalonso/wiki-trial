A **plateau** is an area of relatively flat land that stands well above the surrounding terrain, with at least one steep side. Plateaus are sometimes called tablelands. They occur on every continent and cover about a third of the land surface, from small mesas to vast highlands that support whole countries.

## How plateaus form

Plateaus arise in several ways. Slow, widespread **uplift** of the crust can raise a large block without folding it much; the Colorado Plateau in the United States is an example. Layers of **lava** from repeated fissure eruptions can pile up into thick, flat sheets, as in the Deccan Plateau of India. Plateaus also form when surrounding land is eroded away, leaving a resistant upland behind, and when two continental plates collide and lift a broad region, as happened in Tibet.

## Notable plateaus

- **Tibetan Plateau**: the highest and largest in the world, at an average elevation above 4,500 metres, often called the "roof of the world". Many of Asia's great rivers rise on or near it.
- **Altiplano**: a high plateau in the central Andes shared by [Peru](../../../world/south-america/peru/) and Bolivia.
- **Ethiopian Highlands**: a large upland in [Ethiopia](../../../world/africa/ethiopia/) that feeds the Blue Nile.
- **Colorado Plateau**: a region of high desert and spectacular [canyons](../canyons/).

## Plateaus and people

The high, flat land of a plateau can be good for grazing and farming, and its climate is cooler than nearby lowlands at the same latitude. Some plateau edges produce dramatic [waterfalls](../../water/waterfalls/) where rivers drop over a scarp. Rivers also cut deep into plateaus, and where they do the result is a [valley](../valleys/) or canyon with layered walls that reveal the rock history.

Plateaus are often confused with [mountains](../mountains/), but the key difference is the broad, level top. A plateau that has been strongly dissected by rivers can look mountainous from the inside.

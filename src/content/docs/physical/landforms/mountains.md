A **mountain** is a landform that rises prominently above its surroundings, usually with steep slopes and a distinct summit. There is no single worldwide definition of how high a mountain must be, but several hundred metres of local relief is a common rule of thumb. Mountains often occur in long chains called **ranges**, and roughly a quarter of the land surface is mountainous.

## How mountains form

Most large ranges result from plate tectonics. When two continental plates collide, the crust crumples and thickens in a process called **folding**, building ranges such as the Himalayas and the Alps. Where an oceanic plate sinks beneath a continent, the overlying edge is pushed up and often accompanied by volcanoes, as in the Andes. Other mountains form where blocks of crust are lifted along faults, as in the Basin and Range region of the western United States. See [volcanoes](../volcanoes/) for mountains built from erupted material.

## Major ranges

- **Himalayas**: the highest range, home to [Mount Everest](../../../places/mount-everest/) at about 8,850 metres, running through [Nepal](../../../world/asia/nepal/), India, China and neighbouring countries.
- **Andes**: the longest continental range, stretching about 7,000 kilometres along the western edge of South America, including [Peru](../../../world/south-america/peru/) and [Chile](../../../world/south-america/chile/).
- **Alps, Rockies and Urals**: major ranges of Europe and North America.

## Altitude and life

Air gets colder with height, so climate changes quickly as one climbs. A single mountain can pass through several vegetation zones, from forest at the base to bare rock and permanent snow at the top. Mountains also act as barriers: moist air forced upward cools and drops rain on the windward side, leaving a dry **rain shadow** beyond, which helps create [deserts](../deserts/).

:::tip[Did you know?]
The tallest mountain measured from base to peak is Mauna Kea in Hawaii, most of which lies below the sea surface.
:::

A **landform** is a natural feature of the solid surface of the Earth. Landforms range from small features such as a cave to continent-spanning ones such as a plateau or a mountain range. They are shaped by two broad sets of processes: internal forces, such as plate movement and volcanism, which build the land up, and external forces, such as running water, ice, wind and waves, which wear it down and redistribute the material.

Understanding landforms helps explain where people live, how they farm and travel, and where hazards such as earthquakes, eruptions and floods are most likely. Landforms also influence climate and ecosystems: a high range can block rain, while a broad plain may support farming over huge areas. Water-related features are covered in the [water section](../water/), and the broader context is on the [physical geography](../) page.

## Articles

- [Mountains](mountains/): high, steep landforms formed by folding, faulting and volcanism.
- [Volcanoes](volcanoes/): openings in the crust through which molten rock and gas escape.
- [Plateaus](plateaus/): elevated, relatively flat areas bounded by steep slopes.
- [Valleys](valleys/): low areas between higher ground, carved by rivers or ice.
- [Canyons](canyons/): deep, narrow valleys with steep walls, often cut by rivers.
- [Deserts](deserts/): dry regions with very little rainfall, covering about a third of the land.
- [Glaciers](glaciers/): large, slow-moving bodies of ice on land.
- [Plains](plains/): broad, low-relief areas of flat or gently rolling land.
- [Islands](islands/): pieces of land surrounded by water, from continental to volcanic.
- [Caves](caves/): natural hollows in rock large enough for a person to enter.
- [Coastlines](coastlines/): the edge where land meets the sea, shaped by waves and tides.

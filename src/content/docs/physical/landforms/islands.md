An **island** is a piece of land, smaller than a continent, that is completely surrounded by water. Islands range from tiny sandbars to Greenland, the largest, at about 2.1 million square kilometres. Several countries consist entirely of islands, including [Japan](../../../world/asia/japan/), [Indonesia](../../../world/asia/indonesia/), [Fiji](../../../world/oceania/fiji/) and [New Zealand](../../../world/oceania/new-zealand/). Groups of islands are called **archipelagos**.

## Types of island

### Continental islands

These were once part of a continent and became separated when sea levels rose or the crust shifted. Great Britain, which was joined to Europe during the last ice age, is an example. Continental islands often share plants and animals with the nearby mainland.

### Oceanic islands

Oceanic islands rise from the deep sea floor and have never been connected to a continent. Most are volcanic, built by eruptions at hot spots or plate boundaries. The Hawaiian Islands and [Iceland](../../../world/europe/iceland/) are examples; see [volcanoes](../volcanoes/). Because they are so isolated, oceanic islands often hold species found nowhere else.

### Coral islands

Coral islands, or **atolls**, are rings of reef built on top of a sinking volcano. The central lagoon is the remains of the old crater area. Many Pacific island groups are made of low coral islands, which makes them vulnerable to rising seas. See [coral reefs](../../biomes/coral-reefs/).

### Barrier and river islands

Waves and currents build long, sandy barrier islands parallel to the coast, while rivers create islands of silt in their channels and [deltas](../../water/deltas/).

## Life on islands

Isolation affects evolution. Island species may become unusually small or large, or lose their ability to fly, and they can be hit hard when new species arrive. Islands also have strong influence on culture and trade, since the sea may be both barrier and highway. A coastline long compared to land area gives island nations extensive marine resources; see [coastlines](../coastlines/) and [seas](../../water/seas/).

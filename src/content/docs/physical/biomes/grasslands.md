**Grasslands** are regions where grasses are the main vegetation and trees are rare. They occur where rainfall is too low for forest but too high for desert, usually between about 250 and 900 millimetres a year, and where fire, grazing and drought keep trees from taking over. Tropical grasslands are covered under [savanna](../savanna/); this page describes the temperate kinds.

## Major grasslands

| Name | Region | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prairie | Central North America | Tallgrass in the wetter east, shortgrass in the drier west |
| Steppe | Eurasia, from Ukraine to [Mongolia](../../../world/asia/mongolia/) | The largest continuous grassland belt |
| Pampas | Argentina, Uruguay and southern Brazil | Fertile plains in [Argentina](../../../world/south-america/argentina/) |
| Veld | Southern Africa | High grassland in [South Africa](../../../world/africa/south-africa/) |
| Downs and tussock | Australia and [New Zealand](../../../world/oceania/new-zealand/) | Grazing lands |

## Soil and life

Grass roots are deep and dense, and decay adds so much organic matter that the soil is dark and rich, among the most fertile in the world. Grazers include bison, pronghorn, wild horses and saiga antelope, with burrowing animals such as prairie dogs and ground squirrels, and birds such as larks and eagles. Grasses regrow from the base after fire or grazing, which helps them survive.

## Use and loss

Because of their soils, most temperate grasslands have been ploughed for wheat, maize and other crops, or used for cattle and sheep grazing. Only a small fraction of the original tallgrass prairie remains. Overgrazing and drought can lead to soil erosion, as in the 1930s Dust Bowl on the [plains](../../landforms/plains/) of the United States.

:::tip[Did you know?]
The Mongolian steppe still supports traditional nomadic herding of horses, sheep, goats, camels and cattle.
:::

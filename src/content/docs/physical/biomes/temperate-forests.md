**Temperate forests** occur in the middle latitudes, where winters are cool and summers warm, and rainfall is spread through the year, typically 750 to 1,500 millimetres. They cover large parts of eastern North America, western and central Europe, eastern Asia and smaller areas of the Southern Hemisphere, including Chile and New Zealand.

## Types

- **Deciduous forest**: broadleaf trees such as oak, beech, maple and birch that drop their leaves in autumn to survive winter. This is the classic forest of western Europe and the eastern United States.
- **Mixed forest**: a combination of deciduous and coniferous trees, found where temperate forest meets the northern [taiga](../taiga/).
- **Temperate rainforest**: very wet, mild forest with giant conifers, on coasts such as the Pacific Northwest, southern [Chile](../../../world/south-america/chile/) and [New Zealand](../../../world/oceania/new-zealand/).
- **Evergreen broadleaf forest**: found in warm, humid areas such as southern Japan and parts of eastern China.

## Structure and life

Temperate forests have fewer tree species than tropical ones, but still form layers: canopy, understorey, shrub layer and forest floor. Spring wildflowers bloom before the canopy closes. Deer, bears, foxes, squirrels and many migratory birds live here. Leaf litter builds fertile brown soils, which are among the best for agriculture.

## Human impact

Because the soil is fertile and the climate pleasant, most original temperate forest has been cleared for farming, towns and timber, especially in Europe and eastern China. Forest cover has recovered in places, such as the eastern United States, as farmland was abandoned. Today much of it is managed woodland, protected reserves or small fragments. Countries such as [France](../../../world/europe/france/), [Poland](../../../world/europe/poland/) and [Japan](../../../world/asia/japan/) retain notable woodlands.

:::note
Autumn colour appears when trees stop making chlorophyll, revealing yellow and orange pigments already in the leaf and, in some species, producing new red pigments.
:::

A **savanna** is a tropical grassland with widely spaced trees and a climate that alternates between a wet season and a long dry season. It is the dominant biome across much of Africa and also occurs in South America, India and northern Australia.

## Climate and vegetation

Savannas usually receive 500 to 1,300 millimetres of rain a year, falling mostly over several months. The rest of the year is dry, and fires are common. Grasses grow fast in the rains and dry to straw in the dry season. Trees such as acacias and baobabs survive with deep roots, thick bark and the ability to shed leaves. This is the Aw type in the Köppen scheme (see [climate zones](../../climate/climate-zones/)), and the rain follows the seasonal movement of the tropical rain belt, as described in [monsoons](../../climate/monsoons/).

## Wildlife

African savannas support the largest land animals: elephants, giraffes, zebras, wildebeest, lions and cheetahs. In the Serengeti and Maasai Mara, about a million wildebeest migrate in a yearly circuit following the rain. Termites, birds and many grazing animals shape the vegetation as much as climate does.

## Fire, people and change

Natural and human-set fires prevent the grassland from turning into forest, and grazing keeps the trees open. Savannas have supported pastoral peoples for thousands of years. Today they face pressure from farming, growing populations, and changing rainfall. Many are protected in national parks, notably in [Kenya](../../../world/africa/kenya/) and [South Africa](../../../world/africa/south-africa/).

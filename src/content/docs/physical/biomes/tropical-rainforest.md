A **tropical rainforest** is a dense evergreen forest that grows in warm regions with heavy rain throughout the year. Rainforests cover only about 6 per cent of Earth's land surface, but they are thought to contain more than half of all land plant and animal species.

## Climate and location

Rainforests lie mostly within 10 degrees of the equator, in the Amazon basin, the Congo basin, Southeast Asia and parts of Central America, West Africa and northeastern Australia. Temperatures stay around 25 to 28 degrees Celsius all year, and annual rainfall usually exceeds 2,000 millimetres. This matches the Köppen group A in the [climate zones](../../climate/climate-zones/).

## Structure

The forest is arranged in layers:

- **Emergent layer**: a few giant trees that rise above the rest, sometimes 60 metres tall.
- **Canopy**: a continuous roof of leaves that captures most sunlight and holds much of the animal life.
- **Understorey**: shade-loving shrubs and young trees.
- **Forest floor**: dark and often sparsely vegetated, where leaves decay rapidly.

Despite the lush growth, most nutrients are held in living plants rather than in the soil, which is thin and poor. When forest is cleared for farming, the soil can lose its fertility within a few years.

## Importance and threats

Rainforests store large amounts of carbon, recycle rain through evapotranspiration and are the source of many medicines and foods. They are cleared for cattle, crops such as soy and palm oil, logging and mining. The [Amazon rainforest](../../../places/amazon-rainforest/) is the largest, spanning [Brazil](../../../world/south-america/brazil/) and eight other countries, while [Indonesia](../../../world/asia/indonesia/) holds extensive forest in Southeast Asia.

:::tip[Did you know?]
The Amazon river system, described in [rivers](../../water/rivers/), moves so much water that the forest and river together shape rainfall across much of South America.
:::

A **coral reef** is an underwater structure built from the calcium carbonate skeletons of colonial animals called corals. Reefs cover less than 1 per cent of the ocean floor but are home to about a quarter of marine species, which is why they are often called the rainforests of the sea.

## How reefs form and where they grow

Reef-building corals live in partnership with tiny algae called zooxanthellae inside their tissue. The algae use sunlight to make food, so reefs need clear, shallow water with enough light, a temperature of about 23 to 29 degrees Celsius, and normal ocean salinity. They are therefore found in tropical seas, mostly between 30 degrees north and south. There are three main types:

- **Fringing reefs**: grow close to a shore.
- **Barrier reefs**: separated from land by a lagoon, like the [Great Barrier Reef](../../../places/great-barrier-reef/) off [Australia](../../../world/oceania/australia/), the largest reef system.
- **Atolls**: rings of reef around a lagoon, often on a sunken volcanic [island](../../landforms/islands/), common in the Pacific, in places such as [Fiji](../../../world/oceania/fiji/).

## Value

Reefs shelter fish, protect coasts from waves and storms, and support fishing and tourism for millions of people. They also provide the sand of many tropical beaches.

## Threats

Warming seas cause **coral bleaching**, in which corals expel their algae, turn white and may die. Other threats are ocean acidification, pollution, overfishing, destructive fishing and coastal development.[^1] Mass bleaching events have struck the Great Barrier Reef several times since 1998.

:::danger
Reefs can recover from a single bleaching event if conditions improve, but repeated heat stress at short intervals can destroy them. Reducing emissions is considered essential to their survival.
:::

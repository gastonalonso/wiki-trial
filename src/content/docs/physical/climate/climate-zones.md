title: Climate zones
description: Climate zones group places with similar temperature and rainfall; the Köppen system recognises five main groups from tropical to polar.
---

A **climate zone** is a region where temperature and precipitation follow a similar yearly pattern. The most widely used classification was devised by the German-Russian climatologist Wladimir Köppen around 1900 and later revised by Rudolf Geiger and others. It uses monthly averages of temperature and rainfall and links them to natural vegetation, which is why the zones match many of the world's [biomes](../../biomes/).

## The main Köppen groups

Köppen's system has five main groups, identified by letters, which are then divided into subtypes.[^1]

| Group | Name | Typical features | Example regions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Tropical | Warm all year, every month above 18 °C; wet or with a wet season | Amazon basin, Congo basin, Indonesia |
| B | Dry | Evaporation exceeds rainfall; hot or cold deserts and steppes | Sahara, Arabian Peninsula, central Australia |
| C | Temperate | Mild winters, warm or hot summers; moderate rain | Western Europe, eastern China, southeastern United States |
| D | Continental | Cold winters and warm summers; large yearly range | Russia, Canada, northern United States |
| E | Polar | Every month below 10 °C; tundra or permanent ice | Greenland, Antarctica, Arctic coasts |

### Subtypes

A second letter describes rainfall. In group A, for example, Af is tropical rainforest, Am is monsoon and Aw is savanna. Group B divides into desert (BW) and steppe (BS). Group C includes Mediterranean (Cs), humid subtropical (Cfa) and oceanic (Cfb) climates. A third letter marks summer heat or winter cold.

## Beyond latitude

The zones broadly run in belts from the equator to the poles, but altitude and ocean currents distort them. A high plateau in the tropics can have a polar-like climate, while the warm North Atlantic keeps parts of [Norway](../../../world/europe/norway/) far milder than other lands at the same latitude. Highland areas are sometimes treated as a separate group because their climate changes rapidly with height.

:::tip[Did you know?]
Because zones depend on averages, they shift as the climate changes. Studies of the Köppen system suggest that tropical and dry zones have expanded and polar zones have shrunk in recent decades.
:::

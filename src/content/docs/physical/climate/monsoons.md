A **monsoon** is a seasonal reversal of the prevailing winds, together with the marked change in rainfall that comes with it. The word comes from the Arabic *mawsim*, meaning season. Monsoons affect more than a billion people, and farming across South and Southeast Asia is timed around them.

## How a monsoon works

In summer, land heats faster than the sea. Air over the continent rises, pulling in moist air from the ocean. As this air rises and cools, it releases heavy rain. In winter the pattern reverses: the land is colder than the sea, cool dry air flows out from the continent, and a dry season follows. The movement of the **Intertropical Convergence Zone**, the belt of rising air near the equator, helps shift these winds north and south through the year.

## Major monsoon regions

- **South Asia**: the best known system. The southwest monsoon usually reaches Kerala in [India](../../../world/asia/india/) around the start of June and spreads north over several weeks. Mountains such as the [Himalayas](../../landforms/mountains/) trap the moisture and add to the rain.
- **East Asia**: summer rains bring the plum rains to [China](../../../world/asia/china/) and [Japan](../../../world/asia/japan/).
- **West Africa**: a summer monsoon pushes the rains north into the Sahel, at the southern edge of the [Sahara](../../../places/sahara/).
- **Australia and Indonesia**: a northern summer monsoon brings the wet season to the tropical north of [Australia](../../../world/oceania/australia/).
- **North America**: a weaker monsoon brings summer storms to the southwestern United States and northwestern [Mexico](../../../world/north-america/mexico/).

## Benefits and hazards

Monsoon rain fills rivers and reservoirs and supports crops such as rice. A weak or late monsoon can cause drought and food shortages, while an intense one can cause floods and landslides. Rivers fed by the rains, such as the Ganges and Brahmaputra, build large [deltas](../../water/deltas/) and are prone to flooding.

:::caution
Monsoon rainfall varies from year to year. It is linked to [El Niño](../el-nino/), and El Niño years have often brought weaker rains to India.
:::

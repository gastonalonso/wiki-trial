**El Niño** is a periodic warming of surface waters in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean. The name, Spanish for "the boy" or the Christ child, was used by Peruvian fishermen because the warm water usually appeared around Christmas. It is one phase of a pattern called the **El Niño-Southern Oscillation**, or ENSO.

## The normal pattern and the change

Normally, trade winds blow from east to west across the Pacific, pushing warm surface water toward Indonesia and Australia. Along the coast of [Peru](../../../world/south-america/peru/), cold water wells up from the deep, bringing nutrients that support rich fisheries. In an El Niño, the trade winds weaken, warm water spreads east, and upwelling fades. Rain follows the warm water, shifting from the western to the central Pacific.

**La Niña** is the opposite phase, with stronger trade winds and unusually cool water in the east. Between the two is a neutral state. Events recur every two to seven years and normally last about a year, though no two are identical.

## Effects around the world

- Peru and Ecuador: heavy rain and flooding along the coast, and a collapse in fish catches.
- Indonesia and eastern [Australia](../../../world/oceania/australia/): drought and increased fire risk.
- India and Southeast Asia: a tendency toward a weaker [monsoon](../monsoons/).
- Southern United States: wetter winters; northern states are often milder.
- Atlantic: fewer hurricanes, because stronger upper winds disrupt them (see [tropical cyclones](../tropical-cyclones/)).
- Global: El Niño years tend to set new records for average global temperature.

:::note
El Niño does not cause any single event; it shifts the odds. Forecasters monitor Pacific sea surface temperatures and winds to give warnings several months ahead.
:::

## Link to the wider ocean

ENSO is an example of how the ocean and atmosphere act as one system. The change in Pacific heat alters [ocean currents](../ocean-currents/), including the Humboldt Current that flows north along the South American coast, and reshapes the circulation of the [oceans](../../water/oceans/) more widely.

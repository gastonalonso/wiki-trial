---
title: Weather and climate
description: Weather is the state of the atmosphere at a moment, while climate is its average behaviour over decades; this page explains the difference and the main drivers.
---

**Weather** is the state of the atmosphere in a particular place and time: temperature, rain, wind, cloud and pressure. **Climate** is the long-term pattern of that weather, usually averaged over at least 30 years. A common summary is that climate is what you expect and weather is what you get.

## What drives the atmosphere

The Sun heats Earth unevenly. The tropics receive more energy than the poles, and the atmosphere and oceans move heat from one to the other. Warm air rises near the equator, flows poleward, cools and sinks in the subtropics, and this circulation creates belts of wind and pressure. Earth's rotation bends these winds, giving the trade winds and the westerlies.

### Local influences

Several factors adjust the pattern in any one place:

- **Latitude**: places near the equator are warm all year; places near the poles are cold.
- **Altitude**: air cools by roughly 6 degrees Celsius for every 1,000 metres of height, which is why [mountains](../../landforms/mountains/) have cold summits even in the tropics.
- **Distance from the sea**: water warms and cools slowly, so coasts are milder than continental interiors.
- **Ocean currents**: warm or cold flows alter coastal temperatures; see [ocean currents](../ocean-currents/).
- **Relief**: mountain ranges force air upward, producing rain on windward slopes and dry rain shadows beyond them.

## Measuring weather and climate

Weather is observed with thermometers, barometers, rain gauges, satellites and radar, and forecast with computer models. Climate is described by averages and extremes of these measurements, such as mean monthly temperature and annual rainfall. Geographers group places with similar averages into [climate zones](../climate-zones/).

:::note[Weather versus climate]
A single cold winter does not disprove a warming climate, just as one hot day does not prove it. Climate is judged from long records, not individual events.
:::

Weather also moves water around the planet, linking it to the [water cycle](../../water/water-cycle/). Rain that falls on a [plateau](../../landforms/plateaus/) or plain feeds the rivers and lakes described elsewhere in this wiki.

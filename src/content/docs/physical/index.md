**Physical geography** is the study of the natural world: the shape of the land, the movement of water, the behaviour of the atmosphere and the living communities that result. It asks where features are found, how they formed and how they change over time. Plate tectonics, erosion, ice, wind and rainfall all leave their mark, and together they explain why a mountain range, a desert or a river delta lies where it does.

This part of the wiki is organised into four sections. Each has its own overview page, which links to every article in the section.

## Sections

- [Landforms](landforms/): the shapes of the land surface, including mountains, volcanoes, plateaus, valleys, canyons, deserts, glaciers, plains, islands, caves and coastlines.
- [Water](water/): oceans, seas, rivers, lakes, waterfalls, wetlands, deltas, estuaries, groundwater and the water cycle that links them.
- [Climate](climate/): the long-term patterns of temperature and rainfall, from climate zones and monsoons to tropical cyclones, El Niño, ocean currents and climate change.
- [Biomes](biomes/): the large ecological regions shaped by climate and landscape, such as rainforests, savannas, forests, tundra, coral reefs and mangroves.

## How the parts connect

The sections are closely linked. Mountains force air upward and create rain on one side and desert on the other. Rivers carry sediment from highlands to build deltas on the coast. Ocean currents move heat around the planet and help set the climate zones that, in turn, decide which biome can grow in a given place.

Some features are described in more detail on the [places](../places/) pages, for example [Mount Everest](../places/mount-everest/), the [Sahara](../places/sahara/) and the [Nile](../places/nile/).

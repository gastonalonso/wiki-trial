**Kenya** lies on the equator in East Africa. Its landscape rises from a tropical coast on the Indian Ocean through dry plains to a highland core, which is divided by the Great Rift Valley. It is well known for its [savanna](../../../physical/biomes/savanna/) wildlife.

## Geography

Mount Kenya is an extinct [volcano](../../../physical/landforms/volcanoes/) and the second-highest peak in Africa, still carrying small [glaciers](../../../physical/landforms/glaciers/) despite its position on the equator. The Rift Valley runs north to south, with lakes such as Turkana, Naivasha and Nakuru on its floor. In the west Kenya touches Lake Victoria, the source region of the [Nile](../../../places/nile/). The north is arid, while the coast has beaches, [mangroves](../../../physical/biomes/mangroves/) and coral.

## Climate

Despite the equator, altitude keeps the highlands mild. The coast is hot and humid, and the north is semi-desert. Most areas have two rainy seasons, the long rains from March to May and the short rains in October to December.

:::note
The great wildebeest migration moves between Tanzania's Serengeti and Kenya's Maasai Mara each year, following the rains.
:::

## Regions and places

- **Nairobi**, the capital and a regional hub.
- **The Maasai Mara**, a savanna reserve in the south-west.
- **The coast**, with the old port of Mombasa.
- **The northern frontier**, dry lands around Lake Turkana.

**Antarctica** is the continent around the South Pole, almost entirely buried under the world's largest ice sheet. It is the coldest, windiest and, on average, highest continent, and it has no permanent human population, only seasonal research stations.

| --- | --- |
| Area | about 14.2 million km² |

| Lowest recorded temperature | about -89 °C, at Vostok Station (1983)[^1] |
| Highest point | Vinson Massif, about 4,892 m |
| Governing framework | Antarctic Treaty System (signed 1959, in force 1961) |
| Permanent population | none |

## Landscape

The continent is divided by the Transantarctic Mountains into East Antarctica, a high plateau of old rock, and West Antarctica, a lower region of islands and troughs under ice. The ice sheet holds roughly 70 percent of the world's fresh water, and if all of it melted, global sea level would rise by around 58 metres. See [glaciers](../../physical/landforms/glaciers/) for how ice flows to the coast, where it breaks off as icebergs and ice shelves. A few volcanoes, such as Mount Erebus, are active ([volcanoes](../../physical/landforms/volcanoes/)).

## Climate

Antarctica is a polar desert: the interior receives very little snowfall, often the equivalent of only a few centimetres of water a year. Cold air drains off the plateau as strong katabatic winds. The coasts and the Antarctic Peninsula are milder, and the peninsula has warmed markedly in recent decades ([climate change](../../physical/climate/climate-change/), [climate zones](../../physical/climate/climate-zones/)). Life is confined mostly to the coast and the surrounding Southern Ocean ([oceans](../../physical/water/oceans/)), where penguins, seals and krill thrive.

:::note[The Antarctic Treaty]
The treaty reserves the continent for peaceful purposes and scientific research, freezes territorial claims and bans military activity and nuclear tests. A 1991 protocol added a ban on mineral extraction.
:::

**Nepal** is a landlocked country in the central Himalayas, between China to the north and India to the south, east and west. Within a width of about 200 km it rises from near sea level in the south to the highest point on Earth, so it contains an unusually wide range of landscapes.

## Geography

Nepal has three east-west belts. The Terai in the south is a flat, fertile lowland that is an extension of the Gangetic [plain](../../../physical/landforms/plains/). The central hills, with the Kathmandu Valley, hold most of the population. In the north the high Himalaya form the border, with [Mount Everest](../../../places/mount-everest/), known in Nepali as Sagarmatha, and many other peaks above 8,000 m. Rivers cut deep [valleys](../../../physical/landforms/valleys/) through the range on their way to the Ganges, and many peaks carry [glaciers](../../../physical/landforms/glaciers/).

## Climate

Climate follows altitude, from subtropical in the Terai to alpine and arctic conditions at the top of the range. The [monsoon](../../../physical/climate/monsoons/) brings most rain between June and September, while the best trekking seasons are the dry spring and autumn.

:::note
The geology is young and active. The 2015 earthquake near Kathmandu was a reminder that the Himalayas are still rising as the Indian plate pushes north.
:::

## Regions and places

- **Kathmandu Valley**, the capital region with historic towns.
- **Khumbu**, the Everest region.
- **Pokhara**, a lake city below the Annapurna range.
- **The Terai**, with wildlife reserves.

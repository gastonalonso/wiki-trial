**Mongolia** is a landlocked country in East and Central Asia, sandwiched between Russia to the north and China to the south. It is one of the most sparsely populated countries in the world, and about half of its people live in the capital and its surroundings. Traditional herding of horses, sheep and other livestock still shapes life across the countryside.

## Geography

Most of the country is high, with an average elevation of around 1,500 m. The west and north-west are mountainous, with the Altai range rising to the highest point, while the centre and east are rolling [grasslands](../../../physical/biomes/grasslands/) known as the steppe. In the south lies the Gobi, a cold [desert](../../../physical/landforms/deserts/) of gravel plains and dunes. Northern forests are part of the Siberian [taiga](../../../physical/biomes/taiga/), and Lake Khövsgöl, in the north, drains toward [Lake Baikal](../../../places/lake-baikal/) in Russia.

## Climate

Mongolia has an extreme continental climate, with long, very cold winters and short, warm summers. Rainfall is low and falls mainly in summer. The occasional severe winter storm called a dzud can kill large numbers of livestock.

:::caution
A dzud, a winter of deep snow or ice that stops animals from grazing, can devastate herding families.
:::

## Regions and places

- **Ulaanbaatar**, one of the coldest national capitals in the world.
- **The Altai**, mountains in the far west.
- **The Gobi**, the southern desert.
- **Khövsgöl**, a large northern lake.

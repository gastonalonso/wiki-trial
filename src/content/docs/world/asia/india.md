**India** forms most of the South Asian subcontinent, a triangle of land that juts into the Indian Ocean. It is the most populous country in the world and has a varied geography, from Himalayan peaks to tropical beaches.[^1]

## Geography

India has three broad zones. In the north the Himalayas form a long wall of [mountains](../../../physical/landforms/mountains/), shared with [Nepal](../nepal/) and others. South of them the Indo-Gangetic Plain, watered by the Ganges and the Indus system, is one of the most fertile and densely settled [plains](../../../physical/landforms/plains/) in the world, and the Ganges and Brahmaputra build a vast [delta](../../../physical/water/deltas/) to the east. The Deccan Plateau fills the peninsula and is bordered by the Western and Eastern Ghats. In the north-west, the Thar [Desert](../../../physical/landforms/deserts/) reaches into Rajasthan.

## Climate

The defining feature is the [monsoon](../../../physical/climate/monsoons/). From June to September moist winds from the Indian Ocean bring most of the year's rain, and the timing of the monsoon decides harvests. Winters are dry and mild in the south and cold in the Himalayas, and spring is very hot on the plains.

## Regions and places

- **The Himalayan states**, such as Himachal Pradesh and Sikkim.
- **The Gangetic plain**, with cities such as Delhi and Varanasi.
- **The western coast**, including Mumbai and the Western Ghats.
- **The south**, with Chennai, Bengaluru and the Kerala backwaters.

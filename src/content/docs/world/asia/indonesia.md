**Indonesia** is the largest archipelagic country in the world, with more than 17,000 islands strung along the equator between the Indian and Pacific oceans. About 6,000 are inhabited, and most of its people live on Java.

## Geography

The country lies where several tectonic plates meet, on the Pacific Ring of Fire, and has more than 100 active [volcanoes](../../../physical/landforms/volcanoes/), together with frequent earthquakes. Major islands include Sumatra, Java, Borneo (shared with Malaysia and Brunei), Sulawesi and the western half of New Guinea, where Puncak Jaya carries some of the last tropical glaciers. The surrounding [seas](../../../physical/water/seas/) are shallow and rich in life.

## Climate and biomes

Indonesia has a hot, humid tropical climate with little seasonal temperature variation. The [monsoons](../../../physical/climate/monsoons/) alternate wetter and drier seasons, and [El Niño](../../../physical/climate/el-nino/) years can bring drought and fires. Large areas are covered by [tropical rainforest](../../../physical/biomes/tropical-rainforest/), and the coasts hold [mangroves](../../../physical/biomes/mangroves/) and [coral reefs](../../../physical/biomes/coral-reefs/) within the Coral Triangle.

:::caution
The 1883 eruption of Krakatoa, in the strait between Java and Sumatra, was one of the deadliest in recorded history. Volcanic hazards remain a major concern for the country.
:::

## Regions and places

- **Java**, densely populated, with Jakarta and Yogyakarta.
- **Sumatra**, with highlands and large forests.
- **Bali**, an island known for terraced rice fields.
- **Papua**, a mountainous region on New Guinea.

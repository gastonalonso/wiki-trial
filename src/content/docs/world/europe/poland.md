**Poland** lies in central Europe, with a coast on the Baltic Sea. Most of the country is part of the Great European Plain, and its terrain is mostly flat or gently rolling, rising to mountains only along the southern border.

## Geography

The northern third is a landscape left by ice-age [glaciers](../../../physical/landforms/glaciers/), with thousands of [lakes](../../../physical/water/lakes/) in the Masurian district and a coastline of sandy beaches, dunes and lagoons. Central Poland is a broad [plain](../../../physical/landforms/plains/) drained by the Vistula and Oder [rivers](../../../physical/water/rivers/), both of which flow north to the Baltic. In the south, the Sudetes and the High Tatras, part of the Carpathians, rise above the lowlands. The Białowieża Forest on the eastern border is one of the last large areas of primeval lowland forest in Europe, part of the [temperate forests](../../../physical/biomes/temperate-forests/).

## Climate

The climate is temperate and changes from oceanic in the north-west to more continental in the east. Winters are cold with snow, summers are warm, and rain falls throughout the year, with a peak in summer. The mountains are colder and wetter.

## Regions and places

- **Masovia**, the region around Warsaw.
- **Pomerania and the Baltic coast**, with Gdańsk.
- **Lesser Poland**, with Kraków, in the south.
- **The Tatra Mountains**, on the border with Slovakia.

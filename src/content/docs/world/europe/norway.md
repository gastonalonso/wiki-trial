**Norway** occupies the western and northern part of the Scandinavian Peninsula and stretches from the temperate south to well within the Arctic Circle. It is long and narrow, with a very long coast, and most people live in the south.

## Geography

Norway's western coast is cut by deep [fjords](../../../physical/landforms/valleys/), which are glacier-carved valleys flooded by the sea, and fringed with thousands of [islands](../../../physical/landforms/islands/). Behind them rise high mountains and plateaus, such as the Hardangervidda, with ice caps including Jostedalsbreen, the largest [glacier](../../../physical/landforms/glaciers/) on the European mainland. Forests cover much of the south and east, and the far north is [tundra](../../../physical/biomes/tundra/). The Svalbard archipelago lies far into the Arctic Ocean.

## Climate

The North Atlantic Drift makes the coast far milder and wetter than other places at similar latitudes; see [ocean currents](../../../physical/climate/ocean-currents/). Inland areas are colder and drier, with snowy winters. North of the Arctic Circle there is a midnight sun in summer and polar night in winter.

:::tip[Did you know?]
The Norwegian coast, if all the fjords and islands are counted, is many times longer than the straight-line distance from south to north.
:::

## Regions and places

- **Oslo and the south-east**, the most populated area.
- **The fjord region**, in the west, around Bergen and Geiranger.
- **Trøndelag**, the central region.
- **Northern Norway**, beyond the Arctic Circle.

**North America** is the third-largest continent, covering roughly 24 million square kilometres. It stretches from the Arctic islands of Canada to the narrow land bridge of Central America, and it is bounded by the Arctic, Atlantic and Pacific oceans. Its landscapes range from polar tundra and boreal forest to temperate plains, deserts and tropical rainforest.

The continent is shaped by a western belt of young mountains, the Rockies, Sierra Madre and Cascades, and by the older, worn Appalachians in the east. Between them lie the vast interior plains drained by the Mississippi and its tributaries. The Great Lakes on the Canada–United States border hold a large share of the world's surface fresh water.

Climates follow latitude and altitude. Hurricanes strike the Atlantic and Gulf coasts, while the Pacific coast is often cool and wet. Human geography is just as varied: three large federal states, Canada, the United States and Mexico, dominate the map, with a string of smaller republics to the south.

The pages in this section describe four countries and the places and landforms they are known for. See also the [human geography overview](../../human/) and the [landforms section](../../physical/landforms/).

## Countries

- [Canada](canada/): the second-largest country by area, with long coastlines, boreal forest and the Canadian Shield.
- [Costa Rica](costa-rica/): a small Central American country with volcanoes, cloud forest and two coasts.
- [Mexico](mexico/): a country of high plateaus, deserts and tropical lowlands between the Sierra Madre ranges.
- [United States](united-states/): a continental country from the Appalachians to the Rockies and the Pacific.

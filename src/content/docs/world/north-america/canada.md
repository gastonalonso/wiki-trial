**Canada** occupies the northern part of North America and is the second-largest country in the world by area. Most of its people live in a belt within a few hundred kilometres of the southern border, while the north is a vast, thinly settled expanse of forest, tundra and Arctic islands.

## Geography

The country's core is the **Canadian Shield**, an ancient rocky plateau scoured by glaciers and dotted with innumerable lakes. To the west rise the Rocky Mountains and the coastal ranges, which include Mount Logan in the Yukon, the highest peak in Canada. See [mountains](../../../physical/landforms/mountains/) for how such ranges form. The central prairies are among the continent's great [grasslands](../../../physical/biomes/grasslands/) and now mainly grow wheat and canola.

Canada has more lakes than any other country, and the Great Lakes, shared with the United States, are among the largest freshwater lakes on Earth (see [lakes](../../../physical/water/lakes/)). Its coastline is the longest of any nation, because it counts the many Arctic islands.[^1]

## Climate and biomes

Winters are long and cold across most of the country. The north lies in the [taiga](../../../physical/biomes/taiga/) and [tundra](../../../physical/biomes/tundra/) zones, where permafrost is widespread. The Pacific coast is milder and very wet, supporting temperate rainforest, while the southern interior has warm summers. Warming is faster in the Arctic than the global average; see [climate change](../../../physical/climate/climate-change/).

:::note[Glaciers]
Canada's mountains and Arctic islands still hold large ice caps and glaciers, described on the [glaciers](../../../physical/landforms/glaciers/) page.
:::

## Regions

The country is divided into ten provinces and three territories. Ontario and Quebec hold the largest populations and the capital region. The Atlantic provinces are shaped by the sea, the Prairie provinces by farming and energy, and British Columbia by mountains and the Pacific. Cities such as Toronto, Montréal and Vancouver are discussed under [cities](../../../human/cities/).

[^1]: The figure depends on how finely the coastline is measured, so published lengths vary widely.

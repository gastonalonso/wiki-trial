Earth's land is conventionally divided into **seven continents**: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania and South America. The divisions are partly physical and partly historical, so the exact boundaries, and even the number of continents, differ between countries and traditions. Europe and Asia, for instance, share a single landmass, and many scholars treat them as one continent called Eurasia.[^1]

This section describes the continents and, for most of them, a selection of countries. Each continent has an overview page that lists every country page in its folder.

## The seven continents

| Continent | Area (million km²) | Population (approx.) | Highest point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asia | about 44.6 | about 4.8 billion | Mount Everest, 8,849 m |
| Africa | about 30.4 | about 1.5 billion | Kilimanjaro, 5,895 m |
| North America | about 24.7 | about 600 million | Denali, 6,190 m |
| South America | about 17.8 | about 440 million | Aconcagua, 6,961 m |
| Antarctica | about 14.2 | no permanent residents | Vinson Massif, 4,892 m |
| Europe | about 10.2 | about 745 million | Mount Elbrus, 5,642 m |
| Oceania | about 8.5 | about 45 million | Puncak Jaya, 4,884 m |

:::note
Antarctica has no permanent population. Only researchers and support staff live there, a few thousand in summer and far fewer in winter.
:::

## Continent overviews

- [Africa](africa/): the second-largest continent, from the Sahara to the Cape.
- [Asia](asia/): the largest continent, home to the Himalayas and most of humanity.
- [Europe](europe/): a densely settled continent of peninsulas, mountains and old cities.
- [North America](north-america/): from the Arctic to Central America.
- [South America](south-america/): the Andes, the Amazon and the southern cone.
- [Oceania](oceania/): Australia, New Zealand and the Pacific islands.
- [Antarctica](antarctica/): the frozen continent around the South Pole.

For how these landmasses relate to oceans and climate, see [oceans](../physical/water/oceans/) and [climate zones](../physical/climate/climate-zones/).

[^1]: Figures vary by how boundaries are drawn, for example whether Oceania includes the whole Pacific or only Australasia, and where Europe ends and Asia begins.

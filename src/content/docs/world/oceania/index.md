**Oceania** is the smallest of the continental regions by land area, but it spreads across an enormous stretch of the Pacific Ocean. It is usually taken to include Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and thousands of islands grouped into Melanesia, Micronesia and Polynesia. Australia alone makes up most of the land.

The region's geography is a contrast between a continental landmass and oceanic islands. Australia is low, old and largely dry, with a vast interior and a green rim along the east, south and north coasts. New Zealand sits on the boundary of two tectonic plates, which gives it mountains, [volcanoes](../../physical/landforms/volcanoes/) and earthquakes. Many Pacific islands are either volcanic peaks or low coral atolls built up on [coral reefs](../../physical/biomes/coral-reefs/).

Climates are mostly tropical or temperate, with arid conditions across inland Australia. Cyclones affect the tropical north and the islands, and rising seas are a concern for the lowest atolls ([climate change](../../physical/climate/climate-change/)).

The pages in this section cover three countries. See also [islands](../../physical/landforms/islands/), [oceans](../../physical/water/oceans/) and the [Great Barrier Reef](../../places/great-barrier-reef/).

## Countries

- [Australia](australia/): a continent-sized country of desert interior, tropical north and long coasts.
- [Fiji](fiji/): a Pacific archipelago of volcanic and coral islands in Melanesia.
- [New Zealand](new-zealand/): two main islands of mountains, glaciers, geothermal fields and farmland.

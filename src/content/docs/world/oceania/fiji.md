**Fiji** is an island country in the South Pacific, about 2,000 kilometres north of New Zealand. It consists of more than 300 islands, of which about a hundred are inhabited, scattered across a broad stretch of ocean. Most of the population lives on the two largest, Viti Levu and Vanua Levu.

## Geography

The two main islands are volcanic in origin, with rugged, forested interiors and fertile river valleys that run toward the coast. Many of the smaller islands are either volcanic remnants or low coral islands encircled by lagoons ([islands](../../../physical/landforms/islands/)). Fringing and barrier [coral reefs](../../../physical/biomes/coral-reefs/) surround much of the coast, and mangrove stands line sheltered shores ([mangroves](../../../physical/biomes/mangroves/)).

## Climate

Fiji has a tropical maritime climate, with warm temperatures through the year and a wetter season from about November to April. The windward south-east sides of the main islands are wet, while the leeward north-west is drier and is where sugar cane is grown. Fiji lies in the South Pacific cyclone belt, and [tropical cyclones](../../../physical/climate/tropical-cyclones/) are an annual risk during the wet season.

:::danger
Low-lying coastal communities in Fiji face both cyclone surges and rising sea level, and some villages have already been relocated inland. See [climate change](../../../physical/climate/climate-change/).
:::

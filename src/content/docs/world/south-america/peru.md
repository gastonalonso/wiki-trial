**Peru** lies on the Pacific coast of South America and is one of the continent's most geographically varied countries. Three zones run roughly parallel from west to east: a narrow coastal desert, the high Andes, and the vast lowland rainforest of the Amazon basin. It was the heart of the Inca Empire.

## Geography

The coastal strip, or *costa*, is a dry desert crossed by rivers flowing from the Andes, which create fertile valleys where much of the country's agriculture and population are concentrated. The **Andes** form the highland *sierra*, with snow-capped peaks, deep valleys and high plateaus ([mountains](../../../physical/landforms/mountains/), [valleys](../../../physical/landforms/valleys/)). Huascarán is the highest peak in the tropics.

East of the mountains, the *selva* slopes down into the [Amazon rainforest](../../../places/amazon-rainforest/). The Amazon River itself begins from sources in the Peruvian Andes. In the southeast, Lake Titicaca, shared with Bolivia, is one of the highest large lakes in the world ([lakes](../../../physical/water/lakes/)).

## Climate and biomes

The coast is dry and often covered by mist, because the cold Humboldt Current cools the air above it. Every few years warm [El Niño](../../../physical/climate/el-nino/) conditions bring heavy rain and disrupt fisheries. In the mountains, temperatures depend on altitude, with a pronounced dry season from about May to October. The lowland [tropical rainforest](../../../physical/biomes/tropical-rainforest/) is hot, wet and extremely rich in species.

:::tip
Machu Picchu, an Inca citadel high in the Andes, sits at about 2,400 metres above sea level, on a ridge between two peaks.
:::

## Regions and cities

Lima, on the coast, holds about a third of the population ([cities](../../../human/cities/)). Cusco, once the Inca capital, is the gateway to the Sacred Valley. Quechua and Aymara are widely spoken in the highlands ([languages](../../../human/languages/)).

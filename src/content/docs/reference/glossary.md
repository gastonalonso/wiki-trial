This glossary defines common terms used across the wiki. Where a page covers a term in depth, the term links to it.

## A

**[Aquifer](../../physical/water/groundwater/)** is a layer of rock or sediment that holds and transmits groundwater. Wells and springs draw water from aquifers.

**Archipelago** is a group or chain of islands, usually close together in a sea or ocean. Japan, Indonesia and the Philippines are archipelagos; see [islands](../../physical/landforms/islands/).

**Atoll** is a ring-shaped coral reef or chain of small islands surrounding a lagoon. Most atolls form on top of sunken volcanoes in tropical oceans; see [coral reefs](../../physical/biomes/coral-reefs/).

## B

**Basin** is a low-lying area that collects water or sediment. The word can describe a river's drainage area, an ocean basin or a depression in the land.

**[Biome](../../physical/biomes/)** is a large community of plants and animals shaped by climate, such as tundra, savanna or tropical rainforest.

**[Border](../../human/borders/)** is a line that separates one country or region from another. Borders may follow rivers and mountains or be drawn as straight lines.

## C

**[Canyon](../../physical/landforms/canyons/)** is a deep valley with steep sides, usually cut by a river. The Grand Canyon is a well-known example.

**[Climate](../../physical/climate/weather-and-climate/)** is the typical pattern of weather in a place over many years, usually at least thirty. It differs from weather, which describes conditions on a given day.

**[Climate zone](../../physical/climate/climate-zones/)** is a broad region with similar temperature and rainfall, such as tropical, dry, temperate, cold or polar.

**[Coastline](../../physical/landforms/coastlines/)** is the line where land meets the sea. It includes beaches, cliffs, bays and headlands.

**Continent** is one of the Earth's major land masses. By the common count there are seven: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia (Oceania), Europe, North America and South America.

**Contour line** is a line on a map joining points of equal height above sea level. Lines close together show a steep slope; see [maps and projections](../maps-and-projections/).

**[Coral reef](../../physical/biomes/coral-reefs/)** is an underwater ridge built from the limestone skeletons of corals. Reefs form in warm, shallow, clear water and support a great variety of marine life.

**Crust** is the thin, rocky outer layer of the Earth. Continental crust is thicker than oceanic crust.

**[Cyclone](../../physical/climate/tropical-cyclones/)** is a large rotating storm with low pressure at its centre. Tropical cyclones are called hurricanes in the Atlantic and typhoons in the western Pacific.

## D

**[Delta](../../physical/water/deltas/)** is a fan-shaped area of land formed where a river deposits sediment as it reaches a sea or lake. The Nile and Mississippi deltas are large examples.

**[Desert](../../physical/landforms/deserts/)** is a region that receives very little rainfall, generally under about 250 mm a year. Deserts can be hot, like the Sahara, or cold, like parts of Antarctica.

**Dune** is a hill or ridge of sand built by the wind. Dunes may move slowly across the land.

## E

**Ecosystem** is a community of living things interacting with one another and with their physical surroundings. A pond, a forest or a coral reef can each be an ecosystem.

**[El Niño](../../physical/climate/el-nino/)** is a periodic warming of the surface water in the eastern tropical Pacific. It changes weather patterns in many parts of the world.

**Equator** is the imaginary line around the Earth at 0 degrees latitude, halfway between the poles. It divides the planet into the northern and southern hemispheres; see [latitude and longitude](../latitude-and-longitude/).

**Erosion** is the wearing away of rock and soil by water, wind, ice or gravity. The material removed is often carried elsewhere and deposited as sediment.

**[Estuary](../../physical/water/estuaries/)** is the wide mouth of a river where fresh water mixes with salt water from the sea. Estuaries are affected by tides.

## F

**Fault** is a crack in the Earth's crust along which rocks have moved. Earthquakes occur when stress along a fault is suddenly released.

**Fjord** is a long, narrow, steep-sided inlet of the sea formed when a glacier carved a valley that was later flooded. Norway has many; see [coastlines](../../physical/landforms/coastlines/).

**Floodplain** is flat land beside a river that is covered by water when the river overflows. Its soils are often fertile.

## G

**[Glacier](../../physical/landforms/glaciers/)** is a large mass of ice that moves slowly under its own weight. Glaciers form where more snow falls than melts.

**[Grassland](../../physical/biomes/grasslands/)** is a biome dominated by grasses, with few trees. Examples include the prairies of North America and the pampas of South America.

**[Groundwater](../../physical/water/groundwater/)** is water stored beneath the land surface in soil and rock. It supplies springs, wells and the flow of many rivers.

## I

**Iceberg** is a large piece of freshwater ice that has broken off a glacier or ice shelf and floats in the sea. Most of its mass lies below the water.

**[Island](../../physical/landforms/islands/)** is an area of land completely surrounded by water. Islands may be continental, volcanic or built of coral.

**Isthmus** is a narrow strip of land connecting two larger land areas and separating two bodies of water. The Isthmus of Panama is an example.

## L

**Lagoon** is a shallow body of water separated from the sea by a reef, sandbar or other barrier.

**Latitude** is the angular distance of a place north or south of the equator, measured in degrees. Lines of latitude run east to west; see [latitude and longitude](../latitude-and-longitude/).

**[Lake](../../physical/water/lakes/)** is a body of water surrounded by land. Most lakes hold fresh water, but some are salty.

**Longitude** is the angular distance of a place east or west of the prime meridian, measured in degrees. Lines of longitude run from pole to pole.

## M

**[Mangrove](../../physical/biomes/mangroves/)** is a tree or shrub that grows in salty water along tropical coasts. Mangrove forests protect shorelines and shelter young fish.

**Meridian** is a line of longitude. All meridians run from the North Pole to the South Pole.

**[Monsoon](../../physical/climate/monsoons/)** is a seasonal wind that reverses direction, bringing a wet season and a dry season. It is strongest in South and Southeast Asia.

**[Mountain](../../physical/landforms/mountains/)** is a high, steep landform that rises well above its surroundings. Mountains often occur in ranges.

## O

**Oasis** is a fertile place in a desert where water is available from a spring or well.

**[Ocean](../../physical/water/oceans/)** is one of the Earth's great bodies of salt water. The five oceans are the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern and Arctic.

**[Ocean current](../../physical/climate/ocean-currents/)** is a steady flow of seawater in a particular direction. Currents move heat around the planet and influence climate.

## P

**Peninsula** is a piece of land almost surrounded by water but joined to a larger land mass. Italy and Korea are on peninsulas.

**Permafrost** is ground that stays frozen for at least two years in a row. It is common in [tundra](../../physical/biomes/tundra/) and in the northern forest.

**[Plain](../../physical/landforms/plains/)** is a large area of flat or gently rolling land.

**Plate tectonics** is the theory that the Earth's outer shell is divided into plates that move slowly. Their motion builds mountains, causes earthquakes and creates volcanoes.

**[Plateau](../../physical/landforms/plateaus/)** is an area of high, relatively flat land, often bordered by steep slopes.

**Prime meridian** is the line of 0 degrees longitude, which passes through Greenwich in London. It is the starting point for measuring longitude.

**Projection** is a method for showing the curved surface of the Earth on a flat map. Every projection distorts shape, area, distance or direction in some way; see [maps and projections](../maps-and-projections/).

## R

**Rift valley** is a long valley formed where the Earth's crust is pulling apart. The East African Rift is a major example.

**[River](../../physical/water/rivers/)** is a large natural stream of water flowing toward a sea, lake or another river.

## S

**[Savanna](../../physical/biomes/savanna/)** is a tropical grassland with scattered trees and a long dry season.

**Sea level** is the average height of the ocean's surface, used as the zero point for measuring the height of land.

**[Sea](../../physical/water/seas/)** is a part of the ocean that is partly enclosed by land, or a very large salt lake.

**Steppe** is a dry, treeless grassland in temperate regions, found for example in Mongolia and Central Asia.

**Strait** is a narrow passage of water that connects two larger bodies of water. The Strait of Gibraltar is an example.

## T

**[Taiga](../../physical/biomes/taiga/)** is the vast northern forest of conifer trees, found in Canada, Scandinavia and Russia. It is also called boreal forest.

**[Time zone](../time-zones/)** is a region that keeps the same standard time. The world is divided into zones roughly 15 degrees of longitude wide.

**Tributary** is a stream or river that flows into a larger river. The Amazon has many large tributaries.

**Tropics** is the region of the Earth between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn, near the equator. It is generally warm all year.

**Tsunami** is a series of huge sea waves caused by an earthquake, landslide or volcanic eruption under or near the sea.

**[Tundra](../../physical/biomes/tundra/)** is a cold, treeless biome with permanently frozen soil beneath the surface. It is found in the Arctic and on high mountains.

## V

**[Valley](../../physical/landforms/valleys/)** is a low area between hills or mountains, often with a river or stream flowing through it.

**[Volcano](../../physical/landforms/volcanoes/)** is an opening in the Earth's crust through which molten rock, ash and gas escape. Eruptions can build cones and mountains.

## W

**[Water cycle](../../physical/water/water-cycle/)** is the continuous movement of water between the oceans, the air and the land through evaporation, condensation and precipitation.

**[Waterfall](../../physical/water/waterfalls/)** is a place where a river drops over a vertical or steep edge.

**Watershed** is the area of land that drains into a particular river or lake, also called a drainage basin or catchment. A ridge called a divide separates one watershed from the next.

**[Wetland](../../physical/water/wetlands/)** is an area where the land is covered by shallow water or saturated with water for much of the year, such as a marsh or swamp.

The reference section collects background material that supports the rest of the wiki. Where the [physical geography](../physical/) pages explain how the planet works and the [world](../world/) pages describe particular regions and countries, these pages explain the tools geographers use to locate, map and compare places.

Some pages are short technical primers. Coordinates, projections and time zones answer practical questions: how a location is given as numbers, how a round planet is drawn on a flat page, and why clocks differ from one place to another. The glossary defines terms used throughout the site, and the superlatives page gathers record-holders in tables, such as the longest rivers, the highest mountains and the largest countries.

Many entries link outward to the places, landforms and countries they mention, so the section also works as a short route into the rest of the wiki. Figures given in the tables are approximate and rounded, and where sources disagree, the pages say so.

## Pages in this section

- [Glossary](glossary/): short definitions of terms used across the wiki.
- [Latitude and longitude](latitude-and-longitude/): the grid of coordinates that locates every point on Earth.
- [Maps and projections](maps-and-projections/): how the globe is flattened and what each projection distorts.
- [Time zones](time-zones/): how the world's clocks are organised around Greenwich Mean Time and the date line.
- [Superlatives](superlatives/): world records in tables, from mountains and rivers to the largest countries.

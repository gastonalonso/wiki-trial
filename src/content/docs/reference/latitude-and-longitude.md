---
title: Latitude and longitude
description: Latitude and longitude form the grid of angles used to give the position of any point on Earth, measured from the equator and the prime meridian.
---

**Latitude** and **longitude** are angles that together fix any location on the surface of the Earth. Because the planet is almost a sphere, positions are given as angles from its centre rather than as distances on a flat plane, and the pair of numbers is usually written in degrees.

## Latitude

Latitude measures how far a place is north or south of the **equator**, which is 0 degrees. It runs up to 90 degrees north at the North Pole and 90 degrees south at the South Pole. Lines of equal latitude are called parallels, and they form circles that all run east to west and never meet. A degree of latitude is about 111 kilometres long on the ground almost everywhere.

Several parallels have special names:

| Parallel | Latitude | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arctic Circle | about 66.6° N | Limit of the midnight sun and polar night in the north |
| Tropic of Cancer | about 23.4° N | Northernmost point where the Sun is directly overhead |
| Equator | 0° | Midway between the poles |
| Tropic of Capricorn | about 23.4° S | Southernmost point where the Sun is directly overhead |
| Antarctic Circle | about 66.6° S | Limit of the midnight sun and polar night in the south |

These bands are the basis of the simplest [climate zones](../../physical/climate/climate-zones/): tropical, temperate and polar.

## Longitude

Longitude measures how far east or west a place is from the **prime meridian**, which by international agreement passes through the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, in London. It runs from 0 to 180 degrees east and west. Lines of equal longitude are meridians, and they converge at the poles, so the distance spanned by a degree shrinks from about 111 kilometres at the equator to nothing at the poles. The 180th meridian is the basis of the International Date Line; see [time zones](../time-zones/).

:::tip[Writing coordinates]
Mount Everest lies at roughly 28° N, 87° E. Southern and western values are written with S and W, or with a minus sign in decimal form.
:::

## Why it matters

Satellite navigation today gives a position within a few metres. Map makers use the grid to draw [maps and projections](../maps-and-projections/), and the same angles describe features such as the [Mariana Trench](../../places/mariana-trench/) in the western Pacific.

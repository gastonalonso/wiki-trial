title: Borders

A **border** or boundary is the line that marks the limit of a country or other political area. There are about 195 sovereign states in the world, and the borders between them add up to hundreds of thousands of kilometres. Borders are human creations, but many follow features of the physical landscape.

## Types of borders

- **Natural borders** follow physical features. [Mountains](../../physical/landforms/mountains/) form much of the boundary between France and Spain in the Pyrenees, and between [India](../../world/asia/india/) and [Nepal](../../world/asia/nepal/) in the Himalayas. [Rivers](../../physical/water/rivers/) and [lakes](../../physical/water/lakes/) are also common, such as the Rhine in Europe and the border lakes between Canada and the United States.
- **Geometric borders** are straight lines, often along lines of [latitude and longitude](../../reference/latitude-and-longitude/). The border between [Canada](../../world/north-america/canada/) and the United States for much of its length follows the 49th parallel, and many borders in Africa and the Middle East are straight lines drawn by colonial powers.
- **Cultural borders** follow differences of language or religion.
- **Maritime borders** divide seas; coastal states usually claim 12 nautical miles of territorial sea and a 200-nautical-mile economic zone.

## How borders change

Borders move through war, treaties, independence, unification and the shifting course of rivers. After 1945 and again after 1991, many new states were created, as colonial empires ended and the Soviet Union dissolved. Some borders remain disputed, and some are heavily guarded, while in the European Union's Schengen area most land borders are open to travel.

## Borders and people

A border can cut across ethnic groups, wildlife ranges and watersheds. It affects trade, [migration](../migration/) and even [language](../languages/), since languages often change across lines drawn in history. Lakes and rivers that cross borders require cooperation over water use, as with the [Nile](../../places/nile/).

:::note[Different kinds of line]
Maps may show a border as a firm line even when its position is disputed. See [maps and projections](../../reference/maps-and-projections/) for how this is handled.
:::

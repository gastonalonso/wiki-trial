Roughly **7,000 languages** are spoken in the world today, though the exact number depends on how a language is distinguished from a dialect. They are very unevenly spread: a few languages have hundreds of millions of speakers, while thousands have fewer than 10,000. Papua New Guinea, with about 800 languages, and [Indonesia](../../world/asia/indonesia/) are among the most linguistically diverse countries.

## Language families

Languages that share a common ancestor form a **family**. The largest are:

- **Indo-European**: includes English, Spanish, Hindi, Russian, French and Portuguese, and has the most native speakers of any family.
- **Sino-Tibetan**: includes Mandarin and other Chinese languages, spoken mainly in [China](../../world/asia/china/), and Tibetan.
- **Afro-Asiatic**: includes Arabic, Amharic and Hebrew, across North Africa and the Middle East, including [Egypt](../../world/africa/egypt/) and [Morocco](../../world/africa/morocco/).
- **Niger-Congo**: the largest family by number of languages, including Swahili and Yoruba, spoken in [Nigeria](../../world/africa/nigeria/) and [Kenya](../../world/africa/kenya/).
- **Austronesian**: from Madagascar across Southeast Asia to the Pacific, including Indonesian and Maori.

## Most spoken languages

English has the most speakers when second-language users are counted, at around 1.5 billion, followed by Mandarin Chinese, Hindi, Spanish and French. Mandarin has the most native speakers. Languages spread through trade, empires and migration; English, Spanish and French are widely used because of colonial history, and are often used as official languages alongside local ones.

## Endangered languages

Perhaps 40 per cent of languages are endangered, and a language is estimated to disappear about every two weeks on average. Reasons include the dominance of larger languages in schools, media and work, and [urbanization](../urbanization/) and [migration](../migration/) that move speakers away from home communities. Revival projects, such as those for Maori in [New Zealand](../../world/oceania/new-zealand/), have had some success.

:::tip[Did you know?]
Many languages follow geography. Isolated valleys, islands and rainforest regions tend to have more languages than open plains.
:::

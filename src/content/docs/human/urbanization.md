title: Urbanization
description: Urbanization is the growing share of people who live in towns and cities, which passed 50 per cent worldwide around 2007 and continues to rise.

**Urbanization** is the increase in the proportion of a population living in urban areas. It results from migration from the countryside, natural growth of cities and the reclassification of villages as towns. Around 1800, only a few per cent of people lived in cities. The world passed the halfway mark in about 2007, and today roughly 57 per cent are urban, with projections of about two-thirds by 2050.

## Causes

The main driver is economic change. As farming becomes more productive, fewer workers are needed on the land, while factories and services in towns offer jobs. Cities also offer schools, hospitals and wider choice, which attracts [migrants](../migration/). This process began in Britain during the Industrial Revolution and spread through Europe and North America, and it has now shifted to Asia and Africa.

## Patterns around the world

- **North America, Europe and Latin America**: already more than 75 to 80 per cent urban.
- **Asia**: urbanizing fast, especially in [China](../../world/asia/china/) and [India](../../world/asia/india/), which are adding hundreds of millions of city dwellers.
- **Africa**: the fastest rate of change, with towns growing in countries such as [Nigeria](../../world/africa/nigeria/) and [Ethiopia](../../world/africa/ethiopia/).

The growth of very large cities has created **megacities**, urban areas with more than 10 million people, listed in [cities](../cities/).

## Benefits and problems

Urbanization can lift incomes and reduce the space people use per head. It can also produce informal settlements without secure housing or services, strain water supplies, and increase pollution. Cities built on coasts and [deltas](../../physical/water/deltas/) face growing flood risk from sea level rise and [tropical cyclones](../../physical/climate/tropical-cyclones/). Urban areas are also warmer than their surroundings, an effect called the **urban heat island**.

:::caution
Rapid growth is often faster than planning. Cities that do not expand housing, transport and water together can see slums and congestion grow.
:::

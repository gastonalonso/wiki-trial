Human geography studies how people live on Earth and how they shape and are shaped by the places they inhabit. Where physical geography describes [landforms](../physical/landforms/), [water](../physical/water/), [climate](../physical/climate/) and [biomes](../physical/biomes/), human geography looks at the patterns that people create: where they settle, how they build cities, which languages they speak, where they draw borders and why they move.

The two fields are closely connected. People tend to live where land is fertile, water is available and the climate is mild, such as river valleys and coastal plains, and they avoid deserts, high mountains and polar regions. At the same time, human activity now changes the environment on a global scale, from farming and cities to [climate change](../physical/climate/climate-change/).

The pages in this section describe the world's people in broad terms, using rounded figures. For specific nations, see the [world](../world/) section, and for lasting examples of human and natural landmarks, see [famous places](../places/).

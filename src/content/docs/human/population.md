The **world population** passed 8 billion in late 2022, according to United Nations estimates. It took until about 1800 to reach the first billion, but only about 12 years to grow from 7 to 8 billion. Population studies, called **demography**, look at how many people live in a place and how that number changes through births, deaths and migration.

## Distribution

People are spread very unevenly. More than half of humanity lives in Asia, and roughly a third in just [China](../../world/asia/china/) and [India](../../world/asia/india/). Densities are highest in fertile river valleys, [deltas](../../physical/water/deltas/) and coastal [plains](../../physical/landforms/plains/), and lowest in [deserts](../../physical/landforms/deserts/), polar regions and high [mountains](../../physical/landforms/mountains/). Antarctica has no permanent residents.

## How populations change

Growth depends on birth and death rates. Many countries pass through a **demographic transition**: death rates fall first because of better food and medicine, causing rapid growth, and birth rates then fall as incomes rise and women gain education and work. Several countries, including [Japan](../../world/asia/japan/) and much of Europe, now have ageing and shrinking populations, while growth is fastest in sub-Saharan Africa, including [Nigeria](../../world/africa/nigeria/).

:::note
The UN projects that world population will peak at roughly 10 billion in the 2080s, and then level off or decline slowly.
:::

A **city** is a large and permanent human settlement with dense housing and a concentration of trade, government and services. Definitions vary from country to country, and the population threshold can range from a few thousand to tens of thousands. Geographers often prefer the **metropolitan area**, which includes the built-up suburbs that surround a central city.

## Origins and location

The first cities appeared about 5,000 to 6,000 years ago in Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley and along the [Nile](../../places/nile/), made possible by farming surpluses. Many early cities grew on [rivers](../../physical/water/rivers/), which gave water, fertile soil and transport. Others grew at natural harbours on the [coast](../../physical/landforms/coastlines/), at river crossings or near resources. Today most large cities lie near the sea or a river.

## Functions and challenges

Cities are centres of industry, finance, culture and innovation, and they offer more efficient services per person than scattered settlement. They also face housing shortages, congestion, pollution, flood risk and inequality. Many are growing quickly, as explained in [urbanization](../urbanization/).

:::tip[Did you know?]
Cities cover only about 1 to 3 per cent of Earth's land, yet they are home to more than half of its people.
:::
